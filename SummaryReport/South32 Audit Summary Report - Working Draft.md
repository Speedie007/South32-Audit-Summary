# South32 Wessels Mine Audit Summary Report — Working Draft

> **Working draft for contextual integration and review.**
>
> This document implements the accepted section-specific context developed for the Main Shaft Head Sheaves, Cable Belt Sheaves and Product Spillage workstreams. Detailed inspection evidence, measurements, photographs, classifications, drawings and implementation-level actions remain in the dedicated source reports.

## 1. Executive Summary

Inspection & Specification Services cc (ISS) completed a series of condition and product-containment audits at South32 Wessels Mine. The work covered three independent subject areas: the Main Shaft Head Sheaves, the Cable Belt sheave system, and Product/Material Spillage across the inspected conveyor transport route. These subjects should be read together as part of the same audit-summary exercise, but no causal relationship should be inferred between them unless specifically supported by the underlying evidence.

The Main Shaft Head Sheaves were found to be serviceable within the inspection scope. Minor groove wear was recorded, but no current repair or replacement intervention is indicated. The principal requirement is continued six-monthly condition monitoring so that future change can be assessed against the present baseline.

The Cable Belt inspections identify two areas requiring corrective engineering attention: the Angle Station and the Main Drive. At the Angle Station, the rope path and non-zero fleet-angle condition are consistent with repeated off-centre rope/groove interaction, asymmetric groove wear, increased localized rope stress and adverse lateral bearing loading. The remedial philosophy is to correct the underlying rope-path geometry rather than only replace worn components, with a controlled proof-of-concept on a representative rope path before wider rollout where appropriate. At the Main Drive, the required approach is to formally measure the rope-path and sheave/guide geometry, define the necessary correction, restore alignment and renew worn rope-contact components as a coordinated intervention. The Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves remain serviceable and primarily require condition monitoring, with particular attention to the front-sheave groove-base scoring.

The Product Spillage audit identifies a material-containment problem expressed through several mechanisms. Fines accumulation was present throughout the inspected conveyor ecosystem, with carryback the strongest recurring system mechanism, although not the sole proven cause of every fines deposit. Coarse/mixed-product loss is more localized. The principal engineering response is therefore to reduce recurring loss at source rather than rely on housekeeping alone. CV09 is the proof location for the preferred cleaner-and-containment standard. Once that function is demonstrated under representative operation, relevant underground and surface head/discharge locations should be systematically reviewed and progressively brought to the same functional standard. W10 is the principal reference for localized side-skirt/spill-seal containment, with Sample Conveyors 2 and 3 included in the early high-impact side-containment workstream after their prerequisite feed/loading/tracking conditions are addressed.

Across the audit, the common implementation principle is to verify the actual condition before final design, correct the underlying mechanism where it can be established, prove representative operating performance, and only then close the action or scale the solution.

## 2. Audit Scope and Summary-Report Purpose

The purpose of this summary is to explain the collective meaning of the issued audit reports and present the principal engineering implications and remedial philosophy in a form suitable for management, engineering, maintenance, operations and project/procurement planning.

The summary does not replace the dedicated reports. Those reports remain the technical source for detailed inspection evidence, measurements, photographs, classifications, conceptual drawings, action registers and implementation-level requirements.

The audited subjects are:

- Main Shaft Head Sheaves;
- Cable Belt Main Drive Sheaves;
- Cable Belt Angle Station Sheaves;
- Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves;
- Product/Material Spillage across the inspected underground and surface conveyor/process route.

The Main Shaft lift system is independent of the Cable Belt system. The Product Spillage workstream addresses the wider conveyor transport/material-handling route and should not be causally linked to the Cable Belt sheave findings without specific evidence.

## 3. Main Shaft Head Sheaves

The Underlay, Overlay and Service Winder head sheaves should be treated collectively at summary level. The inspections found the sheaves acceptable/serviceable within the inspection scope. Minor groove wear was recorded, but the findings do not indicate a current requirement for repair or replacement.

No immediate remedial intervention is therefore proposed. The present inspection condition should be retained as the baseline for future comparison, with six-monthly monitoring used to identify whether groove condition or other relevant wear characteristics are changing over time.

Detailed groove measurements, acceptance-criterion governance and individual sheave evidence remain in the dedicated Main Shaft Head Sheaves report.

## 4. Cable Belt Sheaves

The three Cable Belt inspection locations support one broader Cable Belt system but should retain their local causal distinctions. The Angle Station and Main Drive require corrective engineering attention; the Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves remain serviceable and principally require condition monitoring.

### 4.1 Angle Station Sheaves

The Angle Station operates as an eight-sheave coupled mechanism. The inspection findings indicate that the non-zero fleet-angle/off-centre rope path is a significant contributing mechanism to repeated groove-sidewall contact and asymmetric groove wear. That condition is also consistent with increased localized rope/strand stress and accelerated rope wear.

The same rope-path geometry introduces abnormal lateral and overturning reactions into the sheave/bearing system and is considered a credible contributor to reduced bearing life. It should not, however, be presented as the proven sole cause of any individual bearing failure.

The remedial philosophy is to correct the rope path rather than simply replace worn components. The target is for the rope to enter and leave the relevant sheaves on the intended centre plane, at 0° fleet angle or as close to that condition as practicable. Final geometry requires a formal survey and responsible engineering design.

A controlled proof-of-concept may be implemented on one representative/problematic rope path. The complete intervention should then be verified through groove condition, rope behaviour, bearing response and representative service performance before wider rollout. This pilot approach should not delay condition-based intervention on other components that independently require attention.

### 4.2 Main Drive Sheaves

The Main Drive findings form a related but independent geometry/alignment problem. The asymmetric liner/groove wear is consistent with off-centre or lateral loading through the Drive Wheels and Deflector-Guide arrangement, but the final geometry should be established by measurement rather than inferred from wear alone.

The high-level corrective sequence is:

**measure the existing geometry → define the required correction → correct the sheave/guide positions → renew worn Drive Wheel liners and Deflector-Guide sheaves as a coordinated intervention → verify centred tracking and establish an as-left baseline.**

The Main Drive condition may contribute to rope wear, but it should not be merged with the Angle Station eight-sheave mechanism.

### 4.3 Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves

The four Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves remain serviceable within the inspection scope and do not presently require major corrective intervention.

The rear sheaves were generally in good condition. Groove-base scoring on the front sheaves warrants closer initial monitoring so that groove depth/profile, scoring and any future progression can be assessed against the inspection baseline.

Detailed measurements and monitoring criteria remain in the dedicated Upper Gantry report.

## 5. Product / Material Spillage

### 5.1 System Interpretation

The inspected locations form one connected material-handling ecosystem extending from the underground conveyor route through surface screening/transfer and loading-station areas.

The central interpretation rule is that **the location where material accumulates is not necessarily the location where the initiating loss mechanism occurs**. Residual fines may travel on the return belt and detach downstream; rollers can redistribute adhered material; water can move fines away from the original escape point; and tracking conditions can reduce carrying margin before material is lost elsewhere.

Housekeeping remains necessary for access, recovery and consequence control, but it should not be treated as proof that the initiating source has been corrected. Recurring loss requires source or primary-contributor control where the mechanism can be established.

### 5.3 System-Wide Fines / Cleaner-and-Containment Programme

Fines were the dominant recurring spillage form across the inspected conveyor ecosystem. Their presence is not, by itself, the defect; the engineering concern is the loss of fines from the intended material-handling route.

CV09 should be used to demonstrate the preferred cleaner-and-containment arrangement. The preferred normal configuration is two effective cleaner stages acting while the belt is positively supported by the head pulley, located so that cleaner-removed fines remain within the head/discharge-chute process. The cleaner system should incorporate spring-loaded/self-tensioning wear compensation so that positive, controlled belt contact is maintained as the cleaner elements wear, together with positive travel limitation and practical maintenance access.

Where verified local geometry genuinely prevents two effective stages, one effective pulley-supported self-tensioning cleaner stage may be used as the constrained minimum. This should be treated as an exception rather than the normal system standard.

Successful CV09 verification should trigger the systematic review and progressive implementation of the same functional standard at relevant underground and surface head/discharge locations. The objective is to standardize the required cleaning-and-containment function, not to duplicate identical hardware at every conveyor.

CV06 should be addressed in parallel as an immediate cleaner-function restoration case. The missing blade coverage and displaced support condition should be corrected and the restored cleaner verified under representative operating conditions.

### 5.4 Localized Coarse / Large-Product Containment

Coarse and mixed-product loss requires a different approach because it is not universal across the conveyor route.

W10 is the principal demonstration location for the belt-edge side-skirt/spill-seal concept. The final arrangement should be based on verified loading, belt tracking and local conveyor geometry, with confirmed primary contributors corrected before the side-containment duty is finalized.

Sample Conveyors 2 and 3 should form part of the early high-impact implementation programme. Their final skirt arrangements should follow verification and correction of the relevant feed quantity/variation, product-size distribution, loading position, belt position, tracking/alignment and local congestion/geometry conditions.

Once representative performance is demonstrated, the same side-containment principle may be adapted elsewhere where a comparable recurring lateral-product-loss mechanism and suitable geometry are confirmed. It should not be treated as a universal requirement across the conveyor ecosystem.

### 5.5 Supporting Local Workstreams

**U4 Tripper Car.** Quantify and correct the tracking/alignment condition and verify the tripper over its full normal travel under representative loading. Reassess the one-sided coarse-product accumulation and the separate fines condition after correction. U4 should remain a geometry-specific cleaner exception.

**L3.** Quantify tail-end alignment and live tracking. Correct any confirmed defect and reassess the fines condition separately rather than assuming the alignment issue explains the fines accumulation.

**Screens — water and structure.** The active process-water escape requires planned shutdown investigation to establish the internal source before the final modification is defined. The separate corrosion and cut/notched structural-support findings require competent structural assessment and formal repair/as-left acceptance.

**L1.** Structural deterioration and the evidence of previous/intermittent belt-to-structure contact require separate structural and mechanical assessment. The belt was running clear in the inspected operating condition, so the historical contact evidence should not be presented as proof of continuous current contact.

**CV01 and CV04.** Wet-fines/slurry and water are present, but the initiating source is unresolved. These locations require source investigation without transferring the Screens mechanism to them without evidence.

**Trip/restart and housekeeping.** Temporary product removal associated with trips or restart preparation should remain under controlled handling and approved recovery/reintroduction arrangements. Housekeeping is required for access and recovery, but repeat accumulation should remain feedback to the engineering source-control programme.

### 5.6 Product-Spillage Implementation Sequence

The high-level sequence is:

**prove the principal control concepts and restore obvious cleaner defects → address other high-impact local loss/tracking conditions, including the Sample Conveyors → complete water/structural workstreams → systematically extend demonstrated controls → maintain trip/restart and housekeeping governance.**

The formal Priority 1A–5 labels and detailed action register remain in the dedicated Product Spillage report.

### 5.7 Verification and Close-Out

A physical modification or clean-up is not, by itself, proof of success.

The required close-out philosophy is:

**establish the as-found baseline → implement the corrective intervention → verify performance under representative operation → record the as-left condition and performance → close the action or authorize wider rollout.**

This is particularly important for CV09, W10, Sample Conveyors 2 and 3, CV06, tracking/alignment corrections and water-containment work.

## 6. Integrated Remedial Strategy

The three audited subject areas are independent and should not be forced into one causal chain. They can nevertheless be managed under a common engineering-control philosophy.

The Main Shaft Head Sheaves require monitoring rather than immediate corrective work.

The Cable Belt system requires focused geometry/alignment correction at the Angle Station and Main Drive, with verification before wider application of any repeated modification. The Upper Gantry remains a monitoring workstream.

The Product Spillage programme requires a system-level fines-control rollout after CV09 proves the required function, a targeted side-containment programme centred on W10 and the Sample Conveyors, and concise local workstreams for alignment, water, structure and operational controls.

Across these workstreams, the recurring implementation principles are:

- verify the as-found condition before finalizing corrective design;
- address underlying geometry/source mechanisms rather than only visible symptoms;
- use proof-of-concept where a repeated solution is proposed;
- confirm representative operating performance before wider rollout or close-out;
- retain the dedicated source reports as the detailed engineering and evidence references.

The workstreams may be planned in parallel according to South32 operational, maintenance and project constraints. This summary does not impose a single cross-system priority ranking where the underlying systems are independent.

## 7. Conclusion

The audit does not indicate one uniform condition across all inspected systems. The Main Shaft Head Sheaves are presently serviceable and should remain under routine monitoring. The Cable Belt inspections identify geometry/alignment conditions at the Angle Station and Main Drive that warrant corrective engineering, while the Upper Gantry remains serviceable and suitable for continued monitoring.

The Product Spillage findings indicate a broader material-containment problem. Fines control requires a system-level cleaner-and-containment standard proven at CV09 and then progressively implemented at relevant underground and surface discharge locations. Coarse-product loss requires selective containment, with W10 and the Sample Conveyors forming the principal early side-containment workstream. Supporting water, structural, tracking and operational findings should proceed through their dedicated verification and corrective actions.

The governing approach is therefore to correct the mechanism, verify the result in operation, and scale only what has been shown to work. The dedicated inspection reports should be used for detailed evidence, design inputs, formal action registers and implementation requirements.
