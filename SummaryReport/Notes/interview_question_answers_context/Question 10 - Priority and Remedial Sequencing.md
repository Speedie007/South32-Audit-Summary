## Question Asked

> What should the report say about priority and sequencing?
>
> We’ve established that the report should explain remedial philosophy and, where appropriate, the broad order in which actions should be considered.
>
> At the foundation level, should priority generally be based on a combination of:
>
> - safety significance;
> - serviceability or operational risk;
> - likelihood of further deterioration;
> - impact on production or availability;
> - whether an action addresses a root cause versus only a symptom;
> - ease of proving a remedial concept on a limited section first;
> - dependencies between actions;
> - whether one repair should happen before another to avoid wasted effort;
> - practical maintenance or shutdown constraints?
>
> And should the report avoid rigid numerical ranking unless the source reports already provide one, instead using broader language such as **immediate / early attention / planned intervention / monitor / verify first** where appropriate?

## Contextual Interpretation

Priority and sequencing in the summary report should be based primarily on qualitative engineering and operational logic rather than on a rigid scoring system.

Relevant considerations include:

- serviceability or operational risk;
- impact on production or system availability;
- whether the proposed action addresses a root cause or only a symptom;
- whether the remedial concept can first be proven on a limited section or subset;
- dependencies between remedial actions;
- whether one intervention should precede another to avoid duplicated work, ineffective sequencing, or wasted effort.

The report should avoid assigning numerical rankings unless these already exist in the source reports and are appropriate to retain.

Preferred priority language should be broad and management-oriented, for example:

- **immediate**;
- **early attention**;
- **planned intervention**;
- **monitor**;
- **verify first**.

## Contextual Significance

The report should explain **why** an action should occur earlier or later, rather than merely assigning a number.

Sequencing should be driven by practical remediation logic, operational consequence, root-cause treatment, proof-before-scaling, and dependencies between actions.
