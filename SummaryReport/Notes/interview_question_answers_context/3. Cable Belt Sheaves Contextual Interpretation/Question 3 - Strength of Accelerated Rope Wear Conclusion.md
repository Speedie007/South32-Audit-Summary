## Question Asked

Should the summary state that the Angle Station rope-path geometry is a principal contributor to accelerated Cable Belt rope wear, or use the more cautious wording that the observed geometry and asymmetric groove wear are considered a significant contributing mechanism?

## User Answer

> ti would state it as a componation of the two use the first :the Angle Station rope-path geometry is a principal contributor to the accelerated wear being experienced on the Cable Belt ropes, because the non-zero fleet angle drives repeated sidewall contact, asymmetric groove wear, loss of correct rope support and increased strand/contact stress;  but state it in terms of that it is considered a significant contributing mechanism?

## Accepted Contextual Interpretation

Use a combined formulation.

The summary report should communicate that the Angle Station rope-path geometry is **considered a significant contributing mechanism and a principal contributor** to the accelerated wear being experienced on the Cable Belt ropes.

The preferred technical chain is:

**non-zero fleet angle / offset rope path -> repeated off-centre sidewall contact -> asymmetric groove wear -> progressive loss of correct rope support -> increased localized contact and strand stress -> accelerated rope wear**

A suitable summary-level formulation is:

> The Angle Station rope-path geometry is considered a significant contributing mechanism, and a principal contributor, to the accelerated wear being experienced on the Cable Belt ropes. The non-zero fleet angle causes repeated off-centre sidewall contact, which promotes asymmetric groove wear. As the groove profile deteriorates, correct rope support is progressively reduced, increasing localized contact, strand compression/pinching and associated strand fatigue and wear.

## Contextual Significance

This wording deliberately avoids claiming that the Angle Station geometry is the sole cause of Cable Belt rope wear.

It is strong enough to reflect the observed geometry, recurring asymmetric wear pattern and engineering interpretation in the Angle Station report, while preserving room for other wear contributors elsewhere in the Cable Belt system.

For bearing-life discussion, maintain a separate qualification: the off-centre rope load path can introduce lateral/overturning bearing reactions and is considered a credible contributing mechanism to reduced bearing life, but it should not be presented as the proven sole cause of any individual bearing failure without a dedicated root-cause analysis.
