# Question 2 - Rope Path Geometry and Accelerated Wear Mechanism

## Status
Answered - 2026-09-24

## User Answer
The user explained that at the Angle Station the cable-belt rope enters and exits the sheaves offset from the intended groove centre plane. The tensioned rope then bears against the leading fleet-angle side of the groove and progressively wears into the parent material. The user describes this as the rope effectively behaving like a machine tool and "eating" into the groove as it seeks its natural line of travel.

This produces an irregular/asymmetric groove profile. Once distorted, the groove no longer supports the rope correctly and can promote rope pinching, strand stretching/distortion, concentrated contact, outer-strand fatigue and accelerated rope wear.

The mechanism applies across the coupled Angle Station rope path comprising four Lower Angle Station sheaves and four corresponding Upper Angle Station sheaves. The left-hand cable-belt rope passes through four sheaves on the left side and the right-hand rope similarly passes through four on the right side.

The user also explained that the off-centre rope line of action produces angular/lateral forces and overturning moments through the sheave bearing system. Because the rope load is no longer acting through the intended sheave centre plane, the bearing reactions can become unbalanced and additional side-loading can be introduced. This is considered a contributing mechanism to accelerated bearing wear and shortened bearing life experienced by the mine.

The proposed corrective philosophy is to install engineered entry and exit guide sheaves so that the rope traverses the principal sheaves on the intended groove centre plane. The intended result is to prevent abnormal sidewall wear, reduce aggressive rope/sheave contact, restore correct rope support, and centralize the load path through the sheave bearings.

## Contextual Interpretation
Accepted engineering chain for discussion:

**non-zero fleet angle / offset rope path -> preferential groove-sidewall contact -> asymmetric groove wear -> degraded rope support and aggressive rope/sheave contact -> accelerated rope wear**

Additional consequence:

**off-centre rope line of action -> unbalanced lateral/overturning bearing reactions -> increased bearing side-loading -> accelerated bearing wear / shortened bearing life**

This bearing consequence forms part of the same geometry problem and should be highlighted in the final Cable Belt explanation. It should be described as a significant contributing mechanism rather than as the proven sole cause of any individual bearing failure unless a dedicated root-cause analysis establishes that conclusion.

The final strength of causal wording and the distinction between observed evidence, principal probable cause, contributing mechanism and unverified bearing-failure causation still require explicit confirmation before consolidation.

## Scope Boundary
This eight-sheave explanation applies specifically to the coupled Upper/Lower Angle Station arrangement. Related geometry concerns in the Main Drive section must be treated using the evidence and qualifications in that report rather than automatically assumed to be identical. The separately inspected Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves remain their own local condition subsection.


## Refinement - Bearing Loading Consequence

The accepted Question 2 explanation must explicitly include the bearing-load effect of the offset rope path.

The intended summary logic is:

1. the rope enters/exits the sheave at a non-zero fleet angle;
2. the rope bears against the groove sidewall and produces asymmetric groove wear;
3. the distorted groove reduces correct rope support and contributes to accelerated rope wear;
4. simultaneously, the off-centre rope line of action shifts the load away from the intended sheave centre plane;
5. this introduces lateral and overturning reactions into the shaft/bearing system;
6. the resulting unbalanced bearing loading is considered a contributing mechanism to accelerated bearing wear and reduced bearing life.

This bearing consequence should therefore be stated together with the rope/sheave wear mechanism whenever the Angle Station system-level explanation is summarized.
