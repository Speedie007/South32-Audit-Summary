## Contextual Interpretation

The accepted interpretation is that the Angle Station wear condition is a coupled rope-path geometry, sheave-groove wear, rope-wear and bearing-loading problem.

### Rope-Path and Groove-Wear Mechanism

1. The Cable Belt rope enters and/or leaves the Angle Station main sheaves at a non-zero fleet angle or offset from the intended groove centre plane.
2. The tensioned rope seeks its natural line of travel and therefore bears preferentially against the leading fleet-angle sidewall of the groove rather than remaining centrally supported.
3. The rope then behaves, in the user's description, like a **machine tool**, progressively "eating" into the parent material of the groove sidewall.
4. This creates an irregular/asymmetric groove profile rather than the intended centred and approximately symmetrical wear profile.
5. The altered groove geometry no longer provides the rope with correct continuous support through the intended groove profile.
6. The resulting contact condition can increase localized compression, rope pinching, strand stretching/distortion, outer-strand fatigue and abrasion.
7. The combined mechanism therefore contributes materially to accelerated wear of both the sheave groove and the Cable Belt rope.

### Bearing-Loading Consequence

The same offset rope path also affects the sheave load path.

Because the rope line of action is displaced from the intended sheave centre plane:

1. lateral reaction forces are introduced;
2. an overturning/tilting moment is introduced through the sheave/shaft/bearing system;
3. bearing reactions can become unbalanced;
4. bearing side-loading can therefore increase;
5. this is considered a contributing mechanism to accelerated bearing wear and shortened bearing life.

The user specifically indicated that the mine has experienced shortened bearing life / bearing failures on the Angle Station sheaves. This site-history context should be treated as supporting operational context unless separately verified by maintenance or condition-monitoring records.

### Eight-Sheave Angle Station System

The mechanism applies across the coupled Angle Station rope path comprising:

- four Lower Angle Station sheaves; and
- four corresponding Upper Angle Station sheaves.

The Cable Belt uses a left-hand rope and a right-hand rope.

Each rope passes through four corresponding Angle Station sheaves on its respective side, so the system-level concern is repeated rope/sheave interaction across multiple interfaces rather than a single isolated sheave contact point.

## Accepted Engineering Chain

The principal system-level wear chain is:

**non-zero fleet angle / offset rope path → repeated off-centre groove-sidewall contact → asymmetric groove wear → degraded rope support → increased localized rope/strand stress and abrasion → accelerated Cable Belt rope wear**

The associated bearing chain is:

**off-centre rope line of action → unbalanced lateral/overturning reactions → increased bearing side-loading → accelerated bearing wear / shortened bearing life**

These two consequences form part of the same geometric rope-path problem and should be explained together in the final Cable Belt narrative.

## Proposed Corrective Philosophy

The accepted remedial philosophy is to introduce engineered rope-entry and rope-exit control so that the rope traverses each principal Angle Station sheave on the intended groove centre plane, at 0° fleet angle or as close to 0° as reasonably practicable.

The intended effects are to:

- prevent or materially reduce abnormal groove-side loading;
- prevent recurrence of the asymmetric sidewall wear mechanism;
- restore correct rope support within the sheave groove;
- reduce aggressive rope/sheave interaction and outer-strand wear;
- reduce abnormal lateral/overturning loads on the sheave bearing system;
- provide more balanced bearing loading;
- establish a more stable geometry for future wear trending.

The conceptual guide-sheave arrangements shown in the detailed source report define the functional intent only. Final guide-sheave quantity, location, support arrangement and detailed engineering design remain subject to survey and specialist design verification.

## Causal Wording Boundary

The mechanism should be described strongly enough to reflect the observed geometry and repeated wear pattern, but without overstating proof.

Accepted wording principles:

- the non-zero fleet-angle/off-centre tracking condition is a **principal probable cause** of the recurring asymmetric Angle Station groove wear;
- the Angle Station geometry is considered a **significant contributing mechanism and a principal contributor** to the accelerated Cable Belt rope wear;
- the off-centre load path is considered a **credible/significant contributing mechanism** to shortened bearing life;
- do **not** state that the fleet-angle condition is the proven sole cause of any individual bearing failure unless a dedicated root-cause analysis establishes that conclusion.

## Scope Boundary

This eight-sheave mechanism applies specifically to the coupled Upper/Lower Angle Station arrangement.

Related rope-path geometry concerns in the Main Drive section must be treated using the evidence, qualifications and recommendations of the Main Drive report rather than assumed to be identical.

The separately inspected Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves remain their own local condition subsection and should not be automatically grouped into the Angle Station wear mechanism.

## Contextual Significance for the Summary Report

This is a major system-level explanation for the Cable Belt section.

The final summary report should not merely state that the Angle Station sheaves are worn. It should explain why the observed geometry matters:

- the geometry drives abnormal rope/sheave contact;
- that contact changes the sheave groove;
- the distorted groove then provides poorer rope support;
- rope wear is therefore accelerated;
- bearing loading is also adversely affected.

The detailed measurements, individual defect records, photographs, geometry diagrams and final design details should remain in the source report, while the summary report should present the engineering mechanism and remedial philosophy in clear management-level language.
