## Contextual Interpretation

The Cable Belt section should remain primarily narrative and interpretive.

Formal condition ratings, severity levels and maintenance-priority classifications from the detailed reports should not be repeated routinely in the summary.

They should only be included where a classification materially improves management understanding of:

- relative significance;
- urgency;
- remedial sequencing;
- or the distinction between corrective intervention and monitoring.

Otherwise, the summary should explain the technical meaning and direct the reader to the relevant detailed inspection report for:

- formal condition classifications;
- defect severity;
- maintenance priority;
- detailed measurements;
- action registers;
- supporting photographs;
- engineering sketches;
- and implementation detail.

## Contextual Significance

The summary is not intended to answer every technical question contained in the source reports.

Its purpose is to:

- explain the overall engineering story;
- clarify what the findings mean;
- identify the principal remedial philosophy;
- highlight where management attention is required;
- and guide the reader toward the detailed reports for evidence and implementation-level detail.

The summary should therefore encourage informed use of the detailed reports rather than replace them.

This principle applies particularly to the Cable Belt section because repeating report dashboards, classifications and action registers would undermine the intended distinction between the high-level summary and the detailed inspection reports.
