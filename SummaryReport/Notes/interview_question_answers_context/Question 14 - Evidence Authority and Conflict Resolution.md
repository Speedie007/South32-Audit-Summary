## Question Asked

> What should be considered the authoritative source when there is a conflict or ambiguity?
>
> At some point we may encounter differences between:
>
> - the wording in a detailed report;
> - what was observed on site;
> - what was discussed with personnel;
> - your later contextual interpretation;
> - conclusions that emerge only when multiple reports are considered together.
>
> For the foundation, I want to define the hierarchy we should follow.
>
> Would you agree with a rule broadly like this?
>
> 1. **Measured/recorded evidence in the completed reports** has highest authority for factual condition.
> 2. **Explicit conclusions in the completed reports** govern the section-specific technical findings.
> 3. **Your contextual explanation and professional interpretation** can clarify intent, significance, or relationships where the report wording is incomplete.
> 4. **Combined cross-report synthesis** can support broader conclusions where the evidence collectively justifies them.
> 5. **Informal site discussions or recollections** should be treated as supporting context unless independently documented.
> 6. If there is a genuine contradiction that cannot be resolved, the summary report should acknowledge it rather than silently choose one version.
>
> Is that the hierarchy you want, or would you adjust any part of it?

## Contextual Interpretation

The proposed evidence-authority hierarchy is accepted as the **baseline** for the summary report.

### Baseline Evidence Hierarchy

1. **Measured or recorded evidence in the completed reports**
   - Highest authority for factual condition, measurements, and documented observations.

2. **Explicit conclusions in the completed reports**
   - Primary authority for section-specific technical findings and stated conclusions.

3. **Contextual explanation and professional interpretation**
   - May clarify intent, significance, implications, or relationships where source-report wording is incomplete or where the summary requires a higher-level interpretation.

4. **Combined cross-report synthesis**
   - May support broader conclusions where multiple reports collectively justify a system-level trend, implication, or interpretation.

5. **Informal site discussions or recollections**
   - Should be treated as supporting context unless independently documented.

6. **Unresolved contradictions**
   - Where a genuine contradiction cannot be reconciled, the summary report should acknowledge the uncertainty or conflict rather than silently choosing one version.

### Section-Specific Flexibility

This hierarchy is not intended to be mechanically applied in every section.

During the section-specific interview rounds, the hierarchy may be adjusted where:

- a particular evidence layer is not relevant;
- a different source is more appropriate for a specific type of conclusion;
- a section requires a simpler evidence structure;
- the report narrative would be clearer without explicitly invoking all hierarchy levels.

Any such adjustment should be deliberate and recorded in the section-specific context.

## Contextual Significance

The hierarchy provides a default method for resolving ambiguity and maintaining traceability, while still allowing the final report to adapt to the evidence structure of each audited area.

The baseline principle is:

**Use the strongest documented evidence available, preserve traceability, and do not conceal unresolved conflict.**
