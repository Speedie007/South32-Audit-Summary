## Question Asked

> How should uncertainty and professional judgement be handled?
>
> There will likely be places where the evidence supports a strong interpretation but not absolute certainty.
>
> In those cases, should the summary report:
>
> - use qualified wording such as “indicates,” “suggests,” “is consistent with,” or “is likely associated with”;
> - distinguish clearly between confirmed findings and probable interpretations;
> - identify where further inspection, measurement, testing, or engineering verification would be required;
> - avoid forcing a definitive conclusion where the evidence is incomplete?
>
> Also, when ISS’s experience and professional judgement support an interpretation beyond what can be measured directly, how strongly do you want that judgement expressed?

## Contextual Interpretation

Where the available evidence does not support absolute certainty, the summary report should use qualified technical language such as:

- “indicates”;
- “suggests”;
- “is consistent with”;
- “is likely associated with”.

The report should also identify situations where additional inspection, measurement, testing, or engineering verification would be required before a stronger or more definitive conclusion could be made.

The intention is to preserve technical credibility by matching the strength of the wording to the strength of the available evidence.

## Contextual Significance

The summary report should not overstate certainty.

Where conclusions rely partly on professional judgement or incomplete evidence, the wording should remain appropriately qualified and should identify any additional verification needed to strengthen or confirm the interpretation.
