# Question 16 - Section Detail and Narrative Depth

## Interview Metadata

- **Round:** Report Contextual Foundation
- **Question:** 16
- **Status:** Answered
- **Date recorded:** 2026-09-24

## Question Asked

> What level of detail should the summary report carry for each of the three audited areas?
>
> Should each main section generally be:
>
> - a short high-level overview of perhaps a few paragraphs;
> - a moderately detailed narrative with selected supporting examples;
> - or variable in length depending on how much interpretation is required?
>
> My initial expectation is that **Product Spillage** and **Cable Belt Sheaves** may require more interpretive narrative than **Main Shaft Head Sheaves**, because their remedial logic and combined implications appear broader.
>
> Would you want the report to allow that kind of imbalance in section length, rather than forcing all three sections to be roughly equal?

## User Answer

> generally a short high-level overview of perhaps a few paragraphs; but if required adtional section can be added if it will ad value to the summary report, ie a moderately detailed narrative with selected supporting examples;.

## Contextual Interpretation

The default level of detail for each major audited area should be a **short, high-level overview**, generally a few paragraphs.

However, the report should not enforce a rigid equal-length structure across all three technical sections.

Where additional interpretation would materially improve clarity or management understanding, a section may expand into:

- a moderately detailed narrative;
- additional subsections;
- selected supporting examples;
- selective tables or figures where they genuinely add value.

The amount of detail should therefore be driven by the interpretive needs of the section rather than by formatting symmetry.

## Contextual Significance

The summary report should remain concise by default, but it must not sacrifice clarity merely to keep all three sections the same length.

A section should be allowed to expand where the complexity of the findings, remedial philosophy, system-level implications, or selected examples justify greater explanation.
