## Contextual Interpretation

The terminology listed below should be treated as the preferred baseline vocabulary for the summary report, while allowing additional technical terms where required by context.

### Preferred Names

- **South32 Wessels Mine** — site name.
- **Inspection & Specification Services cc (ISS)** — consultant/inspection organization.
- **Main Shaft Head Sheaves**.
- **Cable Belt Main Drive Sheaves**.
- **Cable Belt Angle Station Sheaves**.
- **Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves**.
- **Product Spillage** or **Material Spillage** — both are acceptable depending on context.

### System Terminology Hierarchy

#### Cable Belt System

**Cable Belt System** refers specifically to the cable belt conveyor system that forms part of the underground conveyor infrastructure.

It is one component of the broader product transport route that moves product from underground toward the surface.

This term should not be used as a synonym for the complete mine-wide conveyor system.

#### Conveyor Transport System

**Conveyor Transport System** refers to the complete conveyor network involved in transporting product, including both:

- underground conveyor infrastructure; and
- surface plant conveyor infrastructure.

This is the broadest system-level term.

#### Conveyor

Where used without further qualification, **Conveyor** will typically refer to the conventional conveyor sections, particularly the surface plant conveyor sections using conventional head/tail pulley arrangements with a drive section at the head.

The underground system may also contain conventional conveyor-belt sections in addition to the Cable Belt System.

Because the term “conveyor” can be context-dependent, the final report should qualify it where ambiguity is possible.

## Contextual Significance

This terminology structure is important because:

- the **Cable Belt System** is only one part of the underground conveying infrastructure;
- the **Conveyor Transport System** is the complete underground-to-surface conveying network;
- **Conveyor** may refer to conventional conveyor sections rather than the Cable Belt System specifically.

The final report should preserve these distinctions to avoid incorrectly applying findings from one part of the transport infrastructure to another.
