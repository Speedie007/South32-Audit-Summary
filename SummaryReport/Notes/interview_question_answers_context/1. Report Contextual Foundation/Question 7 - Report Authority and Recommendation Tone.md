## Contextual Interpretation

The summary report should carry the authority of a **professional technical recommendation**, while remaining an **advisory interpretation for South32's own engineering, maintenance, operational, and project decision-making processes**.

It should therefore not be written as a binding directive or implementation instruction.

The report should communicate:

- what ISS's technical assessment indicates should be considered or done;
- why that remedial direction is justified;
- the broader philosophy behind the recommended response;
- the likely implications of not addressing identified concerns;
- how South32 may use the findings to support its own planning and engineering decisions.

### Treatment of Strong Action Language

Where detailed source reports use direct technical language such as:

- replace;
- repair;
- monitor;
- attend urgently;
- remove from service;
- or similar action terminology,

the summary report should generally translate those actions into a **broader management-level remediation philosophy**.

Direct technical language may still be used where it materially strengthens, clarifies, or supports the management-level interpretation, but it should not become the dominant language mode of the summary report.

The summary report should explain the **logic, priority, and strategic intent** behind remedial actions rather than reproducing the detailed action wording from the source reports section by section.

## Contextual Significance

This answer establishes the intended authority and tone of the final document:

**ISS provides a professional technical recommendation and interpretation; South32 retains engineering and operational decision authority.**

The report should therefore avoid sounding either:

- too weak or noncommittal; or
- like an issued engineering instruction or mandatory execution order.

Its preferred voice is:

**technically reasoned, professionally advisory, management-oriented, and clear about the recommended remedial philosophy.**
