## Contextual Interpretation

The supporting Product Spillage workstreams should each receive a **short, dedicated summary subsection**.

These subsections should preserve the importance of the finding without competing with the two principal management narratives:

1. system-wide fines / cleaner-and-containment control; and
2. localized coarse / large-product side-containment control.

The supporting subsections should cover, as applicable:

- Screens process-water escape;
- Screens structural condition;
- L1 structural deterioration and belt-clearance/history;
- U4 tracking/alignment and one-sided coarse-product loss;
- L3 alignment/tracking concern and separate fines condition;
- trip/restart product-handling controls;
- housekeeping / recovery / reintroduction controls.

## Accepted Summary-Level Treatment

For each supporting workstream, state only:

- **what the issue is;**
- **why it matters;**
- **what verification, assessment or corrective action is required;**
- **where the detailed report should be consulted for evidence and implementation detail.**

The summary should not reproduce the detailed report's complete asset-level evidence, action sequence, photographs, technical qualifications or close-out register unless a particular detail is required for management understanding.

## Contextual Significance

This structure allows the report to:

- retain technically important local findings;
- avoid giving the impression that these items are unimportant;
- preserve the detailed report as the technical evidence source;
- prevent secondary/local workstreams from overpowering the principal report message.

The two dominant Product Spillage narratives remain:

- **system-wide fines control;**
- **targeted coarse-product containment.**
