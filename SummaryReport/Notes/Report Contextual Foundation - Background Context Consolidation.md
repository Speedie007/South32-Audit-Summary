## 1. Audit Background

The audit was conducted by **Inspection & Specification Services cc (ISS)** at **South32 Wessels Mine**.

The work covered three technically distinct audited component sets:

1. **Main Shaft Head Sheaves**
2. **Cable Belt Sheaves**
3. **Product Spillage / Material Spillage**

These three areas belong to the same overall audit engagement but must remain technically distinct in the summary report.

### Main Shaft Head Sheaves

The Main Shaft Head Sheave assessment was a standalone condition assessment focused primarily on **sheave groove profile condition**.

It formed part of the mine's periodic inspection activities associated with safety requirements.

### Cable Belt Sheaves

The Cable Belt Sheave assessment consisted of three independently inspected sections:

- Cable Belt Main Drive Sheaves;
- Cable Belt Angle Station Sheaves;
- Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves.

Each section was reported independently, but the sheaves form part of the supporting sheave infrastructure of the **Cable Belt System** and may therefore be interpreted collectively at system level where appropriate.

### Product Spillage

The Product Spillage investigation was a separate operational/system investigation into material escaping from the broader **Conveyor Transport System**.

The detailed evidence and section-specific findings remain in the dedicated spillage report.

### Separation of Concerns

There is no intended causal or interpretive relationship between the **Cable Belt Sheave condition findings** and the **Product Spillage findings**.

The summary report must not create an artificial technical relationship between them merely because both exist within the wider product transport environment.

## 2. Purpose of the Summary Report

The summary report is **not intended to reproduce or compress the detailed findings** already contained in the completed source reports.

The detailed reports remain the primary records for:
- measurements;
- inspection observations;
- section-specific findings;
- component-level evidence;
- detailed recommendations.

The summary report exists to provide:
- clarity;
- holistic interpretation;
- broader implications;
- remediation philosophy;
- appropriate sequencing logic;
- management-level understanding.

The key distinction is:

**Detailed reports answer:** What was found at each inspected section?

**Summary report answers:** What do those findings mean when considered holistically, and how should the resulting remedial approach be understood?

## 3. Intended Audience

The report is intended for a mixed South32 audience including:

- mine management;
- engineering management;
- maintenance management;
- operational personnel;
- procurement and project personnel who may need to act on recommendations.

The report must therefore remain technically credible while being understandable across management, engineering, maintenance, operational, and implementation disciplines.

## 4. Report Authority

The summary report should be interpreted as a combination of:

- a **professional technical recommendation** describing what ISS believes should be done based on the audit findings; and
- an **advisory interpretation** that South32 can use to support its own engineering, maintenance, operational, and project decisions.

It is not intended to function as:
- a binding directive;
- a detailed maintenance instruction;
- an issued engineering design;
- an implementation order.

South32 retains responsibility for its own engineering and operational decisions.

## 5. ISS Scope and Deliverable Boundaries

### Main Shaft Head Sheaves

ISS was expected primarily to:
- inspect and record condition;
- advise whether equipment could remain in service.

### Cable Belt Sheaves

ISS's role included:
- inspect and record condition;
- diagnose probable causes;
- assess severity and/or risk;
- recommend remedial actions;
- define repair methodology at conceptual/advisory level;
- advise whether equipment could remain in service;
- provide an independent technical opinion.

### Product Spillage

ISS's role included:
- inspect and record condition/circumstances;
- diagnose probable causes;
- recommend remedial actions;
- define remediation methodology at conceptual/advisory level;
- provide an independent technical opinion.

### Explicit Scope Exclusions

ISS was not expected to provide:
- detailed engineering design;
- final repair drawings;
- issued-for-construction drawings;
- implementation supervision.

Conceptual drawings included in source reports are explanatory aids only. They are not to scale and must not be interpreted as final technical specifications, fabrication drawings, construction drawings, or implementation-ready design solutions.

## 6. Commissioning Context Qualification

The contextual drivers discussed during the interview were **not formally communicated to ISS as commissioning reasons**.

They were understood from site conditions and discussions with personnel during the audit.

Contextually:
- Main Shaft Head Sheaves related to periodic/safety inspection requirements;
- Cable Belt Sheaves related to deterioration/failure concern and independent condition assessment;
- Product Spillage related to a known operational problem, recurring spillage/production concerns, independent assessment, and possible maintenance or capital planning.

These must remain qualified contextual statements unless documentary evidence confirms them as formal appointment reasons.

## 7. Evidence and Interpretation Hierarchy

The baseline authority hierarchy is:

1. **Measured/recorded evidence in completed reports**
2. **Explicit conclusions in completed reports**
3. **Contextual explanation and professional interpretation**
4. **Combined cross-report synthesis**
5. **Informal site discussions or recollections**
6. **Explicit acknowledgement of unresolved contradictions**

This hierarchy is a baseline and may be adjusted in a section-specific context where some levels are unnecessary or a different structure is more appropriate.

Any material deviation should be deliberately recorded.

## 8. Evidence-Based Holistic Synthesis

The summary report may derive broader conclusions that are not stated verbatim in any single source report where the combined evidence supports them.

This is particularly relevant to the three Cable Belt Sheave reports, where individually granular findings may collectively reveal a broader system-level trend or implication.

A holistic conclusion is acceptable where:
- it is supported by combined evidence;
- the reasoning is traceable;
- it does not contradict the source reports;
- it is clearly presented as holistic interpretation rather than a direct source finding.

The summary report must avoid both:
- inventing unsupported conclusions; and
- suppressing legitimate system-level conclusions merely because they are not explicitly stated in one source document.

## 9. Treatment of Uncertainty

Where evidence does not support absolute certainty, the report should use qualified language such as:

- `indicates`;
- `suggests`;
- `is consistent with`;
- `is likely associated with`.

Where appropriate, the report should identify the need for:
- further inspection;
- additional measurement;
- testing;
- engineering verification.

The strength of the wording must match the strength of the evidence.

## 10. Remedial Philosophy

The summary report should explain the **philosophy and reasoning behind remedial actions**, not merely repeat technical action wording from the detailed reports.

Where source reports use direct wording such as:
- replace;
- repair;
- monitor;
- attend urgently;

the summary report should generally translate that into a broader management-level remedial philosophy.

Direct technical wording may still be included where it materially supports or clarifies the higher-level interpretation.

### Prove Before Scaling

Where applicable, remedial concepts should generally be approached as:

1. apply the proposed remedial concept to a defined location, section, or subset;
2. verify whether the intended result is achieved;
3. confirm that the approach is effective;
4. scale the philosophy to other relevant sections or components only where justified.

The report should not imply universal effectiveness before a remedial concept has been demonstrated.

## 11. Priority and Sequencing

Priority should be qualitative rather than based on an artificial numerical scoring system.

Relevant considerations include:

- serviceability or operational risk;
- impact on production or availability;
- whether an intervention addresses a root cause or only a symptom;
- ability to prove a remedial concept on a limited section first;
- dependencies between actions;
- whether one intervention should precede another to avoid wasted effort.

Preferred language includes:
- **immediate**;
- **early attention**;
- **planned intervention**;
- **monitor**;
- **verify first**.

The report should explain the reasoning behind priority rather than merely assigning labels.

## 12. Report Exclusions

The summary report should avoid:

- repeating detailed findings already contained in source reports except where brief reference is needed;
- reproducing long technical tables or detailed measurements;
- becoming a maintenance work instruction;
- becoming a detailed engineering design document;
- assigning formal risk ratings unless already established;
- creating artificial links between the three audited areas;
- assigning blame or responsibility to individuals or departments;
- making commercial, contractual, or legal conclusions;
- promising that remedial actions will fully eliminate a problem;
- presenting conceptual drawings as final design solutions.

## 13. Reader Familiarity and Source Referencing

The report may assume that readers have access to the detailed source reports and may either:
- already be familiar with them; or
- not have read them in full.

The summary must therefore carry enough context to remain understandable without reproducing detailed technical evidence.

References should be **minimal but traceable**.

Use:
- a light reference where general direction to a source report is sufficient;
- an explicit report and section reference where a specific technical detail, qualification, or evidence trail needs clarification.

## 14. Writing Style

The writing style should be:

- formal and professional;
- plain English where possible;
- technically precise without being overly academic;
- primarily prose-led;
- supported by selective tables or figures only where they genuinely add clarity.

The report should read as a coherent technical management narrative rather than as a compressed inspection schedule or data-heavy technical appendix.

## 15. Baseline Narrative Flow

The default narrative flow is:

1. **What was inspected**
2. **What the collective findings indicate**
3. **What that means operationally/technically**
4. **What remedial philosophy follows from that**
5. **What should happen first, next, or be verified before scaling**
6. **What the broader implications are going forward**

This is a baseline, not a rigid template. It may be adapted where the nature of a specific section requires a different sequence.

## 16. Terminology

Preferred terminology includes:

- **South32 Wessels Mine**
- **Inspection & Specification Services cc (ISS)**
- **Main Shaft Head Sheaves**
- **Cable Belt Main Drive Sheaves**
- **Cable Belt Angle Station Sheaves**
- **Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves**
- **Product Spillage**
- **Material Spillage**

### Cable Belt System

The **Cable Belt System** refers specifically to the cable belt conveyor system forming part of the underground conveyor infrastructure.

It is one part of the broader product transport route from underground to surface.

### Conveyor Transport System

The **Conveyor Transport System** refers to the complete conveyor network, including underground and surface plant conveyors.

### Conveyor

Where used without further qualification, **Conveyor** generally refers to conventional conveyor sections, particularly surface plant conveyors using conventional head/tail pulley arrangements with a drive section at the head.

The underground system may also include conventional conveyor sections.

Where ambiguity is possible, the term must be qualified.

## 17. Desired Reader End-State

At the end of the summary report, the reader should understand:

1. the overall condition and significance of each audited area;
2. which matters require earlier attention and why, where relevant;
3. the broad remedial philosophy for each area;
4. which actions should be proven or verified before being scaled, where applicable;
5. where further inspection or engineering verification is still required;
6. which detailed reports contain supporting evidence;
7. how South32 can use the findings to support maintenance and project planning going forward, where applicable.

Not every section must address every item. These outcomes should be applied selectively according to technical relevance.

## 18. Narrative Depth

Each major audited area should default to a **short high-level overview**, generally a few paragraphs.

A section may expand where additional explanation materially improves the report through:
- moderately detailed narrative;
- additional subsections;
- selected supporting examples;
- selective tables or figures.

The three technical sections do not need to be equal in length.

Narrative depth should be driven by interpretive value, not formatting symmetry.
