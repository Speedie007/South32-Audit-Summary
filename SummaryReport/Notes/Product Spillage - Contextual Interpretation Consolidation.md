## 1. Governing Summary Purpose

The Product Spillage section is not intended to reproduce the detailed spillage audit.

Its purpose is to explain, at management level:

- the extent of the inspected conveyor/process ecosystem;
- the dominant system-wide and localized material-loss patterns;
- the engineering meaning of those patterns;
- the principal remedial philosophy;
- the proof-before-rollout logic;
- the supporting local workstreams;
- and where the detailed Product Spillage report should be consulted for evidence and implementation detail.

The detailed report remains the authority for photographs, measurements, action-register detail, formal priority labels, conceptual drawings, indicative suppliers, detailed design requirements and close-out records.

---

## 2. Baseline Section Architecture

The Product Spillage summary should use one overall material-containment narrative with three levels.

### Level 1 — Inspection Coverage / System Overview

Provide a concise management-facing matrix covering all inspected conveyor/process groups.

The matrix should be in the main body of the Product Spillage section, not an appendix.

It should be arranged in material-flow order from the underground system through to the surface/end-point sections.

Where the source report does not establish an exact interconnection sequence, retain the report-supported grouping and flag any ordering uncertainty for site confirmation rather than invent a process relationship.

### Level 2 — Two Principal Remedial Narratives

1. **System-wide fines / carryback and cleaner-and-containment control**
2. **Localized coarse / large-product loss and side-containment control**

These are the principal management stories and should receive the greatest narrative emphasis.

### Level 3 — Supporting Local Workstreams

Short dedicated subsections should retain the significance of:

- U4 and L3 tracking/alignment concerns;
- Screens process-water escape;
- Screens structural condition;
- L1 structural deterioration and belt-clearance/history;
- CV01 and CV04 unresolved wet-material/water contributors;
- trip/restart product handling;
- housekeeping, recovery and reintroduction controls.

Each supporting subsection should explain what the issue is, why it matters, what verification/corrective work is required, and where the detailed report should be consulted.

## 3. Governing Interpretation Principle

The summary should prominently state:

> **The location where material accumulates is not necessarily the location where the initiating loss mechanism occurs.**

The summary should also preserve the related control principle:

> **Housekeeping addresses the accumulated consequence; source control aims to reduce recurrence.**

This distinction is fundamental to the spillage report and must appear before the main CV09/W10 remedial narratives.

The summary should not:

- attribute every deposit to a defect at the deposit location;
- assume one common cause for all fines;
- treat a temporarily clean area as proof that the initiating mechanism has been corrected;
- or present housekeeping as the primary engineering solution to recurring source-generated loss.

## 4. System-Wide Fines / Carryback Narrative

Fines accumulation was observed throughout the inspected conveyor ecosystem, although severity and immediate mechanism varied.

Carryback is the strongest recurring mechanism, but it is not to be presented as the proven sole cause of every fines deposit.

The management-level objective is end-to-end material containment:

**remove residual fines before the return run and keep cleaner-removed fines within the intended downstream process route.**

### CV09 Role

CV09 is the proof-of-concept/reference location for the preferred cleaner-and-containment standard.

The preferred normal arrangement is:

- pulley-supported cleaning at the head/discharge location;
- two effective cleaning stages where verified geometry permits;
- spring-loaded/self-tensioning wear compensation to maintain positive, controlled cleaner-to-belt contact;
- positive travel limitation;
- retention of cleaner-removed fines within the head/discharge-chute process;
- practical inspection and maintenance access;
- representative operating verification.

Where verified local geometry genuinely prevents two effective stages, one effective pulley-supported self-tensioning stage may be used as the constrained minimum.

That single-stage arrangement is an exception, not the normal rollout target.

### Rollout Expectation

CV09 is the proof location, not the end-state.

After successful representative-performance verification, the relevant underground and surface conveyor head/discharge locations should be systematically reviewed and progressively brought to the same cleaner-and-containment functional standard.

The summary should state this as the expected programme progression, not as an optional “consider wider application” statement.

The function is standardized; final hardware and installation geometry remain site-specific.

U4 remains the known geometry-specific exception requiring local engineering review rather than automatic duplication of the conventional CV09 arrangement.

### CV06

CV06 should be retained as the clear maintenance/restoration case:

- restore a complete functional cleaner arrangement;
- correct the support/retention condition as required;
- reinstate blade coverage;
- incorporate suitable adjustment/wear-compensation improvements;
- verify performance under representative operation.

## 5. Localized Coarse / Large-Product Side-Containment Narrative

Coarse/mixed-product loss is not universal and must remain location-specific.

### W10

W10 remains the principal demonstration/reference application for belt-edge side skirting/spill sealing.

The final arrangement should be developed around verified loading, belt tracking and local geometry, with confirmed primary contributors corrected before the containment system is finalized.

### Sample Conveyors 2 and 3

Sample Conveyors 2 and 3 should form part of the **early high-impact side-containment workstream**, rather than being presented as distant optional applications.

Before final skirt geometry is defined, the relevant feed quantity/variation, product-size distribution, loading position, belt position/tracking/alignment and local geometry conditions should be quantified and corrected or adjusted where required.

### Wider Applicability

After the W10 principle has demonstrated satisfactory representative performance, the same functional side-containment principle may be adapted where comparable recurring lateral coarse-product loss and suitable local geometry are confirmed.

Side skirting is not a universal conveyor-system requirement.

The summary matrix may identify:

- explicitly recommended / early implementation;
- report-supported future applicability;
- candidate for site review;
- monitor / review;
- not presently indicated.

Any later site/user-identified candidate must be identified as such until the local mechanism and geometry are verified.

## 6. Management-Facing Inspection Matrix

The matrix should cover all 23 controlled inspection groups from the issued Product Spillage report, including conveyor and process sections where no side-skirt intervention is currently indicated.

Accepted baseline columns:

1. **Inspected section / conveyor**
2. **Underground or surface**
3. **Dominant spillage type observed**
4. **Principal mechanism / concern**
5. **Primary remedial philosophy**
6. **Fines / cleaner-control status**
7. **Side-skirt status**
8. **Detailed-report reference**

The two remedial-status columns must remain separate so the system-wide fines-control programme is not visually overshadowed by the localized side-skirt programme.

### Fines / Cleaner-Control Status

Baseline categories may include:

- Pilot / reference location
- Upgrade required
- System-standard review / rollout applicable
- Geometry-specific exception / local design required
- Not a primary cleaner-control location

### Side-Skirt Status

Baseline categories may include:

- Explicitly recommended / early implementation
- Report-supported future applicability
- Candidate for site review
- Monitor / review
- Not presently indicated

Exact wording may be refined during document formatting.

## 7. Supporting Workstreams

The following should receive concise dedicated treatment without overpowering the principal fines and coarse-product narratives.

### U4

Quantify and correct tracking/alignment, verify full normal tripper travel under representative loading, and reassess one-sided coarse-product loss and fines separately. Retain U4 as a cleaner-geometry exception.

### L3

Quantify tail-end alignment/live tracking and correct any confirmed defect. Keep the alignment concern and fines accumulation analytically separate unless later evidence proves a causal relationship.

### Screens — Water

Localize the external water escape, inspect the corresponding internal area during planned shutdown, correct the confirmed source, and verify containment after recommissioning.

### Screens — Structural

Complete a competent structural assessment of corrosion, cuts/notches, section loss, connections and remaining adequacy. Obtain formal repair/as-left acceptance.

### L1

Separate the structural and mechanical questions. Assess concrete/steel deterioration and verify representative belt clearance/tracking. Historical/intermittent contact evidence should not be converted into a claim of continuous contact during the inspected condition.

### CV01 / CV04

Retain wet-fines/slurry and water-source uncertainty. Investigate the material/water source without transferring the Screens mechanism to these locations without evidence.

### Trip / Restart and Housekeeping

Confirm controlled temporary product handling, recovery/reintroduction and applicable procedures.

Housekeeping remains necessary for access and consequence control, but should not replace source correction where recurring loss can be addressed.

## 8. Remedial Sequencing in the Summary

The summary should remain interpretive rather than reproduce the formal Priority 1A–5 action register.

Use a narrative sequence:

**prove the principal control concepts and restore obvious cleaner defects → address high-impact local material-loss and tracking conditions, including the Sample Conveyors → complete water/structural workstreams → systematically extend demonstrated controls → maintain trip/restart and housekeeping governance**

The detailed Product Spillage report should be referenced for formal priorities, detailed action sequences and close-out criteria.

---

## 9. Verification and Close-Out Philosophy

Physical work completion alone is not successful close-out.

Use the sequence:

**as-found baseline → corrective intervention → representative operating verification → as-left condition/performance record → close-out or wider-rollout decision**

This applies particularly to:

- CV09;
- W10;
- Sample Conveyors 2 and 3;
- CV06;
- tracking/alignment corrections;
- water-containment work;
- and other interventions whose success depends on in-service behaviour.

For pilots/reference interventions, wider rollout should follow demonstrated representative performance rather than installation completion alone.

---

## 10. Supplier / Procurement Treatment

Supplier and vendor names should remain mainly in the detailed Product Spillage report.

The summary should describe the required engineering function and remedial philosophy rather than name preferred suppliers.

Detailed component selection, OEM requirements, procurement options and fabrication/design detail remain outside the summary-report role.

## 11. Accepted Overall Management Conclusion

The governing management message is:

> The audit identified a system-wide fines-containment problem together with more localized coarse-product loss and several supporting local mechanical, water, structural and operational issues. The principal remedial strategy is therefore to correct the underlying material-containment mechanisms rather than rely on housekeeping. CV09 should be used to prove the preferred upgraded cleaner-and-containment arrangement, after which the same functional standard should be systematically implemented across relevant underground and surface conveyor head/discharge locations. W10, together with the high-impact Sample Conveyor applications, should establish the side-skirt/spill-seal solution for localized coarse-product loss, with wider application only where comparable loss mechanisms and geometry justify it. The remaining local findings should be addressed through their dedicated verification and corrective workstreams, with the detailed Product Spillage report retained as the implementation and evidence reference.

The wording may be refined for final prose, but this technical meaning and hierarchy are accepted.

## 13. Anti-Drift Rules

Do not:

- reduce the fines programme to an optional future review after CV09;
- imply that every fines deposit has the same sole cause;
- imply that all conveyors require identical cleaner hardware;
- treat one-stage cleaning as the preferred normal arrangement where two effective stages are practicable;
- generalize side skirting to the entire conveyor ecosystem;
- invent additional definite skirt locations not supported by the audit or later user/site confirmation;
- merge U4/L3 alignment mechanisms with unrelated fines mechanisms;
- transfer the Screens water source to CV01/CV04 without evidence;
- treat housekeeping or a temporarily clean area as proof of source correction;
- reproduce the detailed action register, supplier list or fabrication-level design in the summary.


## 14. Post-Consolidation Refinement — Simplified Skirting Matrix

The original eight-column management matrix concept has been superseded by a simpler **skirting-focused** layout following user review.

Primary refinement record:

`SummaryReport/Notes/interview_question_answers_context/4. Product Spillage Contextual Interpretation/Post-Consolidation Refinement 1 - Simplified Skirting Matrix Layout.md`

The revised direction is:

- keep the full inspected population visible, but group it into shorter material-flow tables;
- use only the fields needed to identify coarse-product presence/distribution and side-skirt applicability;
- separate the universal fines/cleaner-control programme from the skirting matrix;
- use concise skirting statuses such as **IMPLEMENT / POSSIBLE / REVIEW / NO**;
- retain additional site-identified candidates as later user/site refinements rather than original audit findings.

This refinement supersedes the earlier requirement for separate fines-control and side-skirt status columns within the same matrix. The accepted system-wide fines-control philosophy itself remains unchanged.
