## 1. Section Role in the Summary Report

The Cable Belt Sheaves section should be presented as **one overall Cable Belt system-level narrative** supported by three physical subsections:

1. Cable Belt Main Drive Sheaves;
2. Cable Belt Angle Station Sheaves;
3. Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves.

These are different physical sections of the same broader Cable Belt infrastructure, each supporting or guiding the Cable Belt ropes through a different part of the system.

They should therefore not be presented as three unrelated report summaries.

At the same time, system-level synthesis must not erase technically important local distinctions. The Main Drive, Angle Station, and Upper Gantry findings remain different in mechanism, severity, remedial significance, and required follow-up.

## 2. Overall Management Position

The accepted draft management-level position is:

> The Cable Belt sheave inspections identify two areas requiring corrective engineering attention—the Angle Station and Main Drive—while the Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves remain serviceable and primarily require condition monitoring. Across the two problem areas, rope-path geometry and alignment are key contributors to abnormal rope/sheave contact, asymmetric wear and reduced component life. The Angle Station additionally shows a significant relationship between non-zero fleet angle, irregular groove wear, accelerated Cable Belt rope wear and adverse bearing loading. Remedial work should therefore address the underlying geometry rather than only replace worn components, with the Angle Station suitable for a controlled proof-of-concept before wider rollout.

This is the accepted **draft position**, not immutable final prose. Once the complete summary report is compiled, wording may be refined for flow, proportion, and consistency with the Main Shaft, Product Spillage, and holistic concluding sections, provided that the accepted technical meaning is preserved.

## 3. Management-Level Significance Hierarchy

The three Cable Belt subsections should not receive equal remedial emphasis.

The accepted hierarchy is:

- **Angle Station:** significant system-level rope-path and wear mechanism requiring corrective engineering intervention.
- **Main Drive:** significant physical alignment / rope-path geometry concern requiring investigation and coordinated corrective work.
- **Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves:** no comparable major defect identified; principally a condition-monitoring and wear-trending area.

The summary should make this distinction clear without routinely repeating the detailed formal condition dashboards or priority codes.

## 4. Angle Station — Core Engineering Interpretation

The Angle Station should be interpreted as a coupled rope-path geometry, sheave-groove wear, rope-wear, and bearing-loading problem.

The relevant system comprises four Lower Angle Station sheaves and four corresponding Upper Angle Station sheaves.

The Cable Belt uses a left-hand rope and a right-hand rope. Each rope passes through four corresponding Angle Station sheaves on its respective side. The system concern is therefore repeated rope/sheave interaction across multiple interfaces, rather than a single isolated contact point.

### 4.1 Rope-Path and Groove-Wear Mechanism

The accepted engineering chain is:

**non-zero fleet angle / offset rope path → repeated off-centre groove-sidewall contact → asymmetric groove wear → degraded rope support → increased localized rope/strand stress and abrasion → accelerated Cable Belt rope wear**

The contextual explanation is:

1. the rope enters and/or leaves the Angle Station main sheaves offset from the intended groove centre plane;
2. the tensioned rope seeks its natural line of travel and bears preferentially against the fleet-angle sidewall;
3. in the user's description, the rope effectively behaves like a **machine tool**, progressively "eating" into the parent material of the groove sidewall;
4. this creates an irregular/asymmetric groove profile rather than centred, approximately symmetrical wear;
5. the distorted groove no longer provides correct continuous support to the rope;
6. the resulting contact can increase localized compression, rope pinching, strand stretching/distortion, abrasion, and outer-strand fatigue;
7. the mechanism therefore contributes materially to accelerated wear of both the sheave groove and the Cable Belt rope.

The supporting photographs supplied during the interview were used to clarify this mechanism visually and are recorded in the historical Question 2 context.

### 4.2 Strength of the Rope-Wear Conclusion

The accepted summary wording principle is that the Angle Station rope-path geometry is:

- **a significant contributing mechanism**; and
- **a principal contributor**

to the accelerated wear being experienced on the Cable Belt ropes.

This wording is deliberate. It is strong enough to reflect the observed geometry, recurring asymmetric groove wear, and engineering interpretation, while avoiding a claim that the Angle Station geometry is the sole cause of all Cable Belt rope wear.

A suitable summary-level formulation is:

> The Angle Station rope-path geometry is considered a significant contributing mechanism, and a principal contributor, to the accelerated wear being experienced on the Cable Belt ropes. The non-zero fleet angle causes repeated off-centre sidewall contact, which promotes asymmetric groove wear. As the groove profile deteriorates, correct rope support is progressively reduced, increasing localized contact, strand compression/pinching and associated strand fatigue and wear.

## 5. Angle Station — Bearing-Loading Consequence

The offset rope path also affects the load path through the sheave, shaft, and bearing system.

The accepted bearing chain is:

**off-centre rope line of action → unbalanced lateral/overturning reactions → increased bearing side-loading → accelerated bearing wear / shortened bearing life**

The contextual interpretation is that displacement of the rope line of action away from the intended sheave centre plane can:

- introduce lateral reaction forces;
- introduce an overturning/tilting moment;
- create unequal bearing reactions;
- increase bearing side-loading;
- contribute to accelerated bearing wear and shortened bearing life.

The user identified shortened bearing life / bearing failures as mine operating-history context relevant to this mechanism.

However, the summary must retain a causal boundary:

- the off-centre load path may be described as a credible or significant contributing mechanism to shortened bearing life;
- it must **not** be presented as the proven sole cause of any individual bearing failure unless a dedicated root-cause analysis establishes that conclusion.

## 6. Angle Station — Corrective Philosophy

The accepted remedial philosophy is to correct the **rope path**, not merely replace damaged components while leaving the geometric driver unchanged.

The functional objective is engineered rope-entry and rope-exit control so that the rope traverses each principal Angle Station sheave on the intended groove centre plane at 0° fleet angle, or as close to 0° as reasonably practicable.

The intended effects are to:

- reduce abnormal groove-side loading;
- prevent or materially reduce recurrence of asymmetric sidewall wear;
- restore correct rope support in the sheave groove;
- reduce aggressive rope/sheave interaction and outer-strand wear;
- reduce abnormal lateral/overturning bearing loading;
- provide a stable geometry for subsequent condition trending.

Conceptual guide-sheave arrangements in the detailed Angle Station report define the **functional intent only**. Final quantity, location, support arrangement, and detailed engineering remain subject to quantitative survey and specialist design verification.

## 7. Angle Station — Proof Before Scaling

A key summary-level management message is the **controlled proof-of-concept option**.

The summary should not imply that the preferred approach is necessarily to modify all Angle Station rope paths simultaneously.

The accepted staged logic is:

**select a representative or historically problematic rope path → implement the complete corrective concept on that path → verify geometry and centred tracking → establish post-correction rope/groove/bearing baselines → trend performance over a meaningful operating period → confirm that the irregular wear mechanism and service-performance objectives have improved → expand to the remaining paths where justified**

The detailed report provides approximately **6–12 months** as the proof-of-concept trend-verification period where the staged route is selected.

The purpose of the pilot is to demonstrate that the correction achieves its intended outcome, including improvement in:

- groove wear behaviour;
- rope wear behaviour;
- bearing vibration/temperature trends;
- sheave and rope service performance.

This does not defer condition-based intervention on any other sheave that deteriorates outside mine-defined limits.

## 8. Main Drive — Independent Alignment Narrative

The Main Drive should be treated independently from the Angle Station.

Although both sections show related geometry-driven wear themes, they should not be merged into one causal mechanism.

The Main Drive emphasis is:

- physical alignment and rope-path geometry through the Drive Wheels and Deflector-Guide Sheaves;
- asymmetric liner/groove deterioration consistent with off-centre or laterally loaded rope travel;
- quantitative survey/verification of the actual alignment and fleet-angle geometry before final corrective repositioning is defined;
- coordinated correction of geometry and renewal of worn rope-contact components.

The resulting off-centre loading and asymmetric contact can contribute to Cable Belt rope wear, but this should be presented as a consequence of the Main Drive alignment condition, not as the same eight-sheave Angle Station mechanism.

## 9. Main Drive — Summary-Level Remedial Philosophy

The Main Drive remedial philosophy should be summarized as:

**measure the actual alignment / rope-path geometry → define the required correction → correct the relevant sheave / guide positions → renew the worn Drive Wheel liners and Deflector-Guide Sheaves as one coordinated intervention → verify centred tracking and establish a new baseline**

The summary should explain the logic behind this sequence:

- measurement must define the actual correction;
- geometry correction and component renewal are interdependent;
- replacing worn components without correcting confirmed geometry may reproduce the wear mechanism;
- correcting geometry while retaining permanently distorted rope-contact profiles may continue to provide poor rope support;
- post-intervention verification is required to demonstrate centred tracking and create a new condition baseline.

The detailed five-phase sequence, individual survey interfaces, action-register items, exact engineering steps, and verification records should remain in the dedicated Main Drive report.

The summary must not become a maintenance work instruction.

## 10. Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves — Concise Monitoring Section

The Upper Gantry subsection should remain concise.

Accepted summary content:

- all four Upper Gantry Guide Sheaves remain serviceable within the inspection scope;
- no immediate remedial intervention is required;
- the rear sheaves are generally in good condition;
- the front sheaves show groove-base scoring that warrants closer initial monitoring;
- future inspections should trend groove depth, groove profile, and visible scoring against the current baseline.

The Upper Gantry section should not receive the same remedial emphasis as the Main Drive or Angle Station sections.

Detailed dimensional readings, exact inspection intervals, individual asset data, photographs, acceptance references, and granular inspection evidence should remain in the dedicated Upper Gantry report.

## 11. Relationship Between the Three Cable Belt Sections

The final Cable Belt narrative should preserve both a **system-level theme** and **local technical independence**.

At system level:

- rope-path geometry and alignment can drive abnormal rope/sheave contact;
- abnormal contact can create asymmetric wear;
- asymmetric wear can adversely affect rope and component life;
- correcting underlying geometry is more important than repeatedly treating only the worn component.

At local-section level:

- the **Angle Station** has the accepted coupled Upper/Lower rope-path, groove-wear, rope-wear, and bearing-load mechanism;
- the **Main Drive** has a separate physical alignment / rope-path geometry problem requiring quantitative verification and coordinated correction;
- the **Upper Gantry** is presently serviceable and primarily a monitoring/trending concern.

Do not automatically transfer a causal conclusion from one subsection to another merely because the wear mechanisms are similar.

## 12. Use of Condition Ratings and Priorities in the Summary

The Cable Belt section should remain primarily narrative and interpretive.

Formal condition ratings, severity levels, and maintenance-priority classifications from the detailed source reports should **not** be repeated routinely.

Use them only when a classification materially improves management understanding of:

- relative significance;
- urgency;
- remedial sequencing;
- or the distinction between corrective intervention and monitoring.

Otherwise, the summary should explain the engineering meaning and direct the reader to the detailed source report for formal classifications and granular evidence.

The summary is intended to encourage informed use of the detailed reports, not replace them.

This section can be more developed than the Main Shaft Head Sheaves section because the Cable Belt findings require substantive technical interpretation.

However, it should still remain a management-readable summary rather than a condensed reproduction of three inspection reports.

A suitable narrative structure is:

1. introduce the three Cable Belt inspection sections as parts of one broader system;
2. state the overall distinction between corrective-intervention areas and the serviceable monitoring area;
